The problem

The truth lives in exactly one place

No tests

220 billion lines of COBOL run in production. Almost none of it has a test suite, a specification, or an author still at the company. The behaviour is real and entirely undocumented.

No answer key

You cannot diff against a spec that was never written. Ground truth has to be manufactured — captured from the running system, then extended to reach the paths production never touches.

No way to say no

A generated candidate looks right long before anyone can prove it is right. Without evidence, sign-off becomes a judgement call, and judgement calls are what boards later read about.

The factory

Four workers. One of them is not a model.

Most agentic platforms are a debating society: several models argue toward consensus. Confidence then becomes a function of how persuasive your models are, and it moves every time one is updated. We built the opposite.

station 01

Planner

Reads the work order and the approved slice of the estate graph. Emits a bounded plan: which files may change, which gates must pass.

sees: graph scope, source capsules
station 02

Builder

Writes the candidate. Every edit passes through a broker that validates it against the declared path allowlist before it lands.

sees: plan, redacted failure
station 03 · not a model

Verifier

Deterministic gates in a digest-pinned sandbox, holding the private holdout. The only station that decides pass or fail.

sees: everything, including the answer key
station 04

Analyst

Turns a failure into a repair direction. Receives the redacted envelope, so it can locate a defect without being handed the answer.

sees: which field diverged, how often
holdout boundary — expected values never cross

Give a generation agent the expected output and it learns to emit the expected output. You ship a system that reproduces your test corpus instead of your business, and every dashboard above it reports green. That failure is invisible until production, which is why the boundary is an allowlist that defaults closed.

Why we publish this

The architecture is public on purpose. In a trust business, an unexplained method is not a moat — it is a reason to be turned down in procurement. Knowing that our judge is deterministic and blind does not let anyone replicate the hard part: manufacturing ground truth for a system that has no tests, and being trusted when the answer is no.

What stays private is the evidence itself — holdout cases, corpora, and your data. Publishing the design is how you hold us to it.

From proof to programme

Independently qualified subsets that compose into one governed pilot

A demo proves one program. A programme needs several technologies to hold together at once, with the dependencies between them declared and checked. Each estate technology is qualified separately, then composed — and the composed package is blocked from dispatch until every constituent claim is satisfied.

What we test for

The failure mode is not a wrong answer. It is a confident empty one.

A verification tool that reports success having verified nothing is invisible, it ships, and the customer finds it when reconciliation breaks. We attack our own comparator on every build to make sure it cannot do that.

Injected defectWhat a naive comparator doesOurs
Candidate writes nothingreports passed, exit 0indeterminate

Fixed-point money becomes floating pointpasses until it compoundsfailed

Each row is a real mutation run against our own code. A test that cannot fail is not evidence, so every guard is verified by breaking the thing it protects and confirming the suite goes red.

Governance

Every permitted difference has an owner and an expiry date

Some differences between two systems are genuinely meaningless — a run timestamp, a padding convention. Waving those through is necessary and it is also how a verification harness gets quietly widened until everything passes. So each one is a controlled entry: a written reason, a named owner, and a review date that fails closed when it lapses.

Honest roadmap

What we have not done yet

No live mainframe baseline

Every equivalence claim in the product is gated pending authorised z/OS capture. That is deliberate, and it is the next value inflection. A verification company that quietly promotes simulated evidence has nothing to sell.

One workload family proven

Breadth across genuinely different estates is unproven. We would rather tell you that now than have you discover it in month three of a programme.

Trust

Designed for estates that cannot take a risk on us

We are asking regulated organisations to let an autonomous system touch their most sensitive code. The architecture assumes you will not extend trust on the strength of a demo.

Read-only by default

Collection is read-only. Live capture requires operator attestation and verified TLS with certificate and hostname validation. Nothing is written back to your estate.

Evidence cannot be laundered

Simulated evidence cannot satisfy an equivalence claim. Replaying a live capture yields recorded, never live — weak evidence cannot be promoted by round-tripping it.

Sandboxed execution

Gates run in a digest-pinned container with a read-only host bind. Work orders are signed, expiring, and replay-safe. Agent identities are short-lived and action-scoped.

Hash-chained audit

Every event binds actor, action, subject, policy decision and evidence receipt to an exact graph identity. A reviewer can replay the evidence behind any release decision.

Your data stays yours

Comparison output contains real record values by design — that is what makes it evidence. Classification, access control and retention on run directories are agreed before the first capture, not after.

No third-party runtime

The verification core has zero external runtime dependencies. For a tool whose product is evidence, the dependency tree is part of what your auditor has to trust.

Getting started

An engagement begins read-only. We take source and, where available, captured input and output from the running system. Nothing is written back to your estate at any point.

# 1 — build a typed graph of the estate
lightyr graph build --source ./estate --out knowledge/graph.json

# 2 — measure what your existing corpus actually reaches
lightyr coverage report --program CBTRN02C --corpus ./captured

# 3 — run one bounded proof cell end to end
lightyr factory run --work-order work-orders/intcalc.json

Step 2 is usually the one that changes the conversation. It answers a question nobody has asked before: how much of this program's decision surface has any test you own ever exercised.

Work orders

A work order is a contract, not a prompt. It declares the paths an agent may touch, the slice of the graph it may read, the gates that must pass, and the timeouts. Scope is a precondition — an agent cannot widen it at run time.

{
  "id": "intcalc-repair",
  "allowed_paths": ["src/interest/**"],
  "graph_scope": ["CBACT04C", "TCATBAL"],
  "gates": [
    { "id": "unit",    "expose_output_to_builder": true  },
    { "id": "holdout", "expose_output_to_builder": false }
  ]
}

expose_output_to_builder defaults to false and must be a real JSON boolean — a quoted "false" is rejected rather than coerced, because in Python it would evaluate as true and silently open the holdout boundary.

Verdicts and exit codes

Three verdicts, and the third is the one most tools do not have.

VerdictExitMeaning
faithful0Every compared field matched under the equivalence policy.
failed1A real divergence, classified by cause and located by field.
indeterminate2Nothing was verified. An operator error, never routed back to a builder as a defect.

The separation of 1 and 2 matters operationally. A failed run means the candidate is wrong. An indeterminate run means the harness could not tell, which is a configuration problem and must not be handed to an agent as "your code is broken."

Normalization ledger

Some differences are genuinely meaningless — a run timestamp, a padding convention. Each permitted one is a controlled entry with a written reason, a named owner, and a review date. The validator fails closed on a missing owner, an expired review, an undocumented rule, or a rule whose text no longer matches the code.

lightyr validate-normalizations --ledger spec/normalizations.json

# NORMALIZATION_REVIEW_EXPIRED:fixed-width-padding:2026-02-22
# NORMALIZATION_GOVERNANCE_MISSING:decimal-canonical:owner
# NORMALIZATION_RUNTIME_MISMATCH:copybook-filler:behavior

Prefer a shape-preserving normalization over an outright ignore. Ignoring a timestamp lets a candidate that never writes one pass; collapsing its digits still asserts format, length and punctuation.

Coverage

We measure arc coverage, not statement coverage. A corpus can execute every line of a validation paragraph and never once take the reject branch — and reject branches are where modernization defects survive to go-live.

Arcs are classified into three populations with three different owners, because blending them produces a number that sends teams to chase the wrong work:

PopulationOwnerAcceptance
Businessthe harnessoutput matches legacy under the policy
ResilienceNFR suitefails safe, no partial commit, alert raised
Infrastructureout of scopedocumented, so it is not re-raised

Fidelity coverage is reported over business arcs only. Where the corpus misses one, we generate the input that reaches it — then run it through the legacy system first, because a synthetic case whose expected output a human decided is not evidence.

CI integration

The comparator is designed to be a gate, so exit codes route directly. Treat 2 as a pipeline error rather than a test failure — it means the run proved nothing.

- name: Differential verification
  run: |
    lightyr compare --expected ref/ --actual out/ --report r.json
    # 0 faithful · 1 divergent · 2 indeterminate

- name: Reject runs that mutate tracked evidence
  run: git diff --exit-code

That second step is worth copying whatever tooling you use. It asserts that a verification run is side-effect free, so a suite can never quietly rewrite the evidence it is checking against.

Questions we get asked

Frequently asked

Do you compete with code-generation platforms?

No. We sit underneath them. Generation is collapsing in price and will keep collapsing — a better model should change your throughput, not your confidence. We are the layer that decides whether generated change is safe to switch on, and we are deliberately agnostic about who wrote it. Several of our engagements verify a candidate produced by someone else's platform.

What do you need from us to start?

Read-only access to source and, for the assessment, enough captured input and output from the running system to be representative. We will tell you if it is not representative — that finding is usually the most valuable output of the first month.

Why does the product refuse to claim equivalence?

Because we have not yet captured an authorised z/OS baseline, and simulated evidence cannot satisfy an equivalence claim. The gate is enforced in code. When you supply the baseline, the claim unblocks. Until then the product shows you a blocked claim, which is the honest state.

How is this different from a golden-master test suite?

A golden master compares against outputs someone chose to capture. We measure which decision paths that capture actually reaches, then manufacture the inputs to close the gaps — usually rejection and error paths, which barely appear in production traffic and are where modernization defects survive to go-live.

Does the comparison report contain sensitive data?

Yes, by design. The expected and actual values are the evidence, and masking them would destroy the artifact. Classification, access control and retention on run directories are agreed with your security team before the first capture. This is a conversation we start early rather than discover in procurement.

What happens to the harness after the programme ends?

It stays. A modernization is a project; behavioural regression is permanent. Most customers keep the harness running against the estate long after the migration, which is when it stops being a cost line and starts being infrastructure.
