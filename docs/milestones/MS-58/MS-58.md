# MS #58 - CloudBank Whole-Application Transaction Wave

> **Status:** Complete
>
> **Release:** 0.58.0
>
> **Date:** 2026-09-02
>
> **Audience:** Customers, partners, executives, architects, delivery teams, and assurance reviewers

## Executive summary

MS #58 - CloudBank Whole-Application Transaction Wave converts a specific part of the LIGHTYEAR modernization approach into a governed, reviewable capability. Places every deployable CloudBank service under one governed migration sequence and prepares the first connected money-movement wave without confusing a complete plan with a completed migration.

## Why this matters to a customer

Places every deployable CloudBank service under one governed migration sequence and prepares the first connected money-movement wave without confusing a complete plan with a completed migration.

The practical result is a narrower, evidence-backed decision instead of a broad modernization claim. The milestone packages capability, proof, and limitations together so commercial, architecture, delivery, security, and assurance teams can review the same record.

## What the milestone delivers

- A complete inventory of all eight root deployable services with every service assigned exactly once to a delivery wave.
- An ordered whole-application plan covering qualified Customer, Account/Transfer transaction core, messaging/authorization, edge/auxiliary services, and production-like operations.
- An exact 17-file Account and Transfer source contract bound to the pinned CloudBank commit and trees.
- A 13-column PostgreSQL 16 mapping for Accounts and Journal tables, identities, constraints, timestamps, foreign keys, and explicit money-precision policy.
- A transaction behavior contract covering success, invalid amounts, insufficient funds, deposit failure, duplicate commands, authorization denial, crash recovery, and journal replay.
- A deterministic recovery state-model rehearsal for value conservation, compensation, idempotency, authorization order, checkpoint resume, and exact journal reconstruction.
- A compatibility ledger that keeps Oracle AQ/JMS and MicroTx LRA replacement blocked on native semantic evidence.
- A signed admission receipt chained to the MS #57 receipt and immutable Oracle and PostgreSQL image identities, plus schemas, launchers, CI, Control Tower status, documentation, and adversarial tests.

## How it differs from earlier work

MS #57 qualified the bounded Customer service. MS #58 accounts for the whole deployable portfolio and chains that result into an admission-ready Customer, Account, and Transfer transaction wave while preserving native execution as later evidence.

Earlier work remains useful evidence, but it is not automatically promoted into this milestone. A successful parser, simulator, local comparison, or generated artifact is not presented as live platform equivalence or production readiness.

## What a customer can do with it

A customer can use cloudbank whole-application transaction wave as a bounded decision and delivery capability. It can be attached to the customer's approved source package, owners, policies, target architecture, acceptance criteria, and authorized evidence.

Unresolved semantic loss, policy decisions, unsupported behavior, and live-evidence requirements remain visible rather than being averaged into one pass/fail statement.

## Evidence and acceptance posture

The milestone is complete as a repository capability because its committed implementation and release record are present. Customer qualification remains claim-specific: only the delivered behaviors listed above are supported, and only at the evidence class recorded by their underlying receipts and gates.

A customer review should confirm:

1. the exact source, graph, policy, fixture, and artifact identities referenced by the evidence;
2. the difference between deterministic development evidence and authorized live evidence;
3. every policy decision, lossy transformation, unsupported behavior, and explicit exclusion;
4. the acceptance gates that passed and the live or production gates that remain blocked; and
5. that later changes have not invalidated the content-addressed evidence.

## Boundaries and claims not made

- The milestone inventories and plans the whole application; it does not claim the whole application has migrated.
- The committed readiness receipt is static admission evidence, not native Account or Transfer execution.
- The recovery rehearsal is a deterministic state model, not a live distributed transaction or broker observation.
- Oracle AQ/JMS and MicroTx LRA replacement remain unresolved until ordering, atomicity, retry, compensation, and recovery pass natively.
- Target Account and Transfer code is not generated by this milestone.
- Production data is not accessed, copied, profiled, or implied.
- Whole-CloudBank equivalence, migration completion, production-data equivalence, and production readiness remain false.

These boundaries are part of the deliverable. They prevent bounded development evidence from becoming an unsupported customer promise.
