## Executive summary

MS #46 - Unified Estate Operator Navigation converts a specific part of the LIGHTYEAR modernization approach into a governed, reviewable capability. Gives an operator one customer-centered graph experience for moving between mainframe and database work while preserving the cross-platform dependencies that explain customer impact.

## Why this matters to a customer

Gives an operator one customer-centered graph experience for moving between mainframe and database work while preserving the cross-platform dependencies that explain customer impact.

The practical result is a narrower, evidence-backed decision instead of a broad modernization claim. The milestone packages capability, proof, and limitations together so commercial, architecture, delivery, security, and assurance teams can review the same record.

## What the milestone delivers

- A customer, technology-scope, and operator-lens context bar that separates engagement, platform focus, and task perspective.
- Unified-estate, mainframe, and database scopes that visually focus the graph without hiding connected cross-platform context; SAP estate and security-vulnerability views are explicitly planned.
- Platform classification and coverage metadata for COBOL, PL/I, DB2, VSAM, IMS, JCL, CICS, assembler, BMS, Java, Oracle, SAP ASE, and future SAP evidence.
- Directed source-to-target tracing plus a related-evidence mode, platform sequence, hop count, evidence class, and runtime limitation.
- First-class IMS DLI statement nodes and ISSUES_DLI, READS_SEGMENT, and WRITES_SEGMENT relationships that resolve source operations through the program's PSB/PCB authorization view.
- One-click source-backed COBOL-to-IMS DLET tracing plus a separate non-customer AUTHUPD1 PL/I-to-DB2 UPDATE reference fixture; the original ACCTPL1 path remains a DB2 read.
- Visible static-source, static-reference-fixture, non-customer, and runtime-not-observed labels for every proven example.
- Search and trace gap states that explain why Oracle and SAP ASE cannot yet participate in an end-to-end graph claim.
- Regression tests for directionality, platform coverage, planned lenses, composite PL/I paths, portable UI controls, and documentation integrity.

## How it differs from earlier work

Earlier graph work made individual perspectives explorable, and MS #43/MS #45 qualified Oracle and SAP ASE adapters outside the graph. MS #46 adds an operator model and evidence-aware path tracing across the unified estate without pretending that standalone adapter evidence is already an end-to-end customer integration.

Earlier work remains useful evidence, but it is not automatically promoted into this milestone. A successful parser, simulator, local comparison, or generated artifact is not presented as live platform equivalence or production readiness.

## What a customer can do with it

A customer can use unified estate operator navigation as a bounded decision and delivery capability. It can be attached to the customer's approved source package, owners, policies, target architecture, acceptance criteria, and authorized evidence.

Unresolved semantic loss, policy decisions, unsupported behavior, and live-evidence requirements remain visible rather than being averaged into one pass/fail statement.

## Evidence and acceptance posture

The milestone is complete as a repository capability because its committed implementation and release record are present. Customer qualification remains claim-specific: only the delivered behaviors listed above are supported, and only at the evidence class recorded by their underlying receipts and gates.

A customer review should confirm:

1. the exact source, graph, policy, fixture, and artifact identities referenced by the evidence;
2. the difference between deterministic development evidence and authorized live evidence;
3. every policy decision, lossy transformation, unsupported behavior, and explicit exclusion;
4. the acceptance gates that passed and the live or production gates that remain blocked; and
5. that later changes have not invalidated the content-addressed evidence.

## Boundaries and claims not made

- The graph proves committed static and declaration relationships; it does not prove that a production transaction executed.
- The COBOL-to-IMS write path proves statically declared DLI mutation intent against an authorized segment; it does not prove that IMS executed or committed the operation.
- The AUTHUPD1 PL/I-to-DB2 write is a bundled reference fixture, not customer source; ACCTPL1 remains read-only.
- Oracle and SAP ASE qualification artifacts are not yet projected into the graph and have no customer integration call edges.
- The bundled customer selector represents the CardDemo reference estate; no customer system is attached.
- SAP application and governed security-vulnerability findings remain planned capabilities.
- Cross-platform production behavior, live equivalence, and production readiness remain false.

These boundaries are part of the deliverable. They prevent bounded development evidence from becoming an unsupported customer promise.
